Why Does Every Company Need a Proper HR Policy in Pakistan?

Faiz Alam Faizi
•May 9, 2026•2 min read

Share this article

A proper HR policy is not merely an administrative document. It is a legal shield that helps a company manage hiring, attendance, salary, leave, misconduct, harassment complaints, termination, confidentiality, and workplace discipline in a fair and documented manner. In Pakistan, companies without clear HR policies often face disputes over salary deductions, late coming, employee misconduct, harassment complaints, data misuse, forced resignation, and wrongful termination. This article explains why every company needs a proper HR policy and how it protects both employer and employee.

This article explains why every company in Pakistan needs a proper HR policy. It is useful for startups, SMEs, corporate companies, factories, schools, clinics, software houses, service businesses, printing businesses, e-commerce companies, employers, HR departments, directors, managers, and employees. A well-drafted HR policy helps define workplace rules before disputes arise. It explains what the company expects from employees and what employees can expect from the company. It also creates a documented structure for attendance, leave, salary, benefits, discipline, harassment complaints, company property, confidentiality, digital evidence, termination, and final settlement. The central principle is simple. A company that runs only on verbal instructions is legally exposed. A company that runs on clear written policies is easier to manage, easier to defend, and easier to grow. For businesses that need legally careful HR policies, employment contracts, disciplinary procedures, harassment mechanisms, confidentiality clauses, or termination documentation, Legal Bridge LLP provides Pakistan-focused legal support through https://legalbridge.pk/.

What Is an HR Policy?

An HR policy is a written set of workplace rules that explains how a company manages its employees. It covers the relationship between employer and employee from appointment to exit. A proper HR policy should not be copied from the internet. It should match the company’s business model, employee structure, legal obligations, workplace culture, and operational needs. In Pakistan, many employment disputes arise because companies work without proper documentation. Employees are hired verbally, salaries are agreed informally, attendance rules are unclear, leave is approved casually, and termination is issued suddenly. A proper HR policy reduces this uncertainty

Why Is an HR Policy a Legal Protection Tool?

An HR policy is a legal protection tool because it creates clarity. If an employee is accused of misconduct, the employer must show what rule was breached. If salary is deducted, the employer must show the basis. If leave is refused, the company should show the policy. If termination occurs, the process should be documented. Without policy, the company may still say that an employee did wrong, but proving it becomes difficult. A written policy helps show that the company acted fairly, consistently, and in accordance with known rules.

Why Are Verbal Rules Dangerous for Companies?

Verbal rules create disputes. One manager may say the employee was warned. The employee may deny it. One supervisor may allow flexible timing. Another may call it misconduct. One employee may be punished for late coming while another is ignored. This is how workplace disputes become legal problems. A proper HR policy removes uncertainty. It tells everyone what the rule is, how it will be applied, and what consequences may follow

How Does an HR Policy Help in Hiring?

A proper HR policy helps the company define hiring procedure, appointment terms, probation period, confirmation process, employee categories, documentation requirements, reporting structure, and background verification. It also helps avoid future disputes about salary, role, benefits, working hours, confidentiality, notice period, and job duties. Every company should connect its HR policy with appointment letters and employment contracts. Why Is an Attendance Policy Important?

Attendance disputes are among the most common workplace issues in Pakistan. Employees may dispute late coming, absence, leave status, half-day deductions, overtime, shift timing, or biometric records. A proper HR policy should explain: Office timings. Grace period, if any. Late coming consequences. Absence reporting procedure. Leave approval process. Emergency leave rules. Remote work rules, if any. Biometric or manual attendance procedure. Salary deduction rules where lawful. Disciplinary consequences for repeated violations. This helps employers avoid arbitrary deductions and helps employees understand their duty.

Why Is a Leave Policy Necessary?

A leave policy protects both sides. Employees need to know how to apply for leave, what leave is available, when leave may be rejected, and what documents are required. Employers need a process to manage absence without disrupting work. A proper leave policy should address annual leave, casual leave, sick leave, emergency leave, maternity-related leave where applicable, unpaid leave, leave approval, leave rejection, and consequences of unauthorized absence. Without a leave policy, absence disputes quickly become disciplinary disputes.

How Does an HR Policy Prevent Illegal Salary Deduction?

Salary deduction is legally sensitive. A company should not deduct salary randomly or emotionally. Deductions should be based on law, contract, attendance record, leave status, policy, or proved liability. A proper HR policy should explain salary payment date, payslip system, deduction rules, late coming deductions, unpaid leave, damage recovery procedure, advance salary adjustment, overtime where applicable, and final settlement. This protects the company from wage claims and protects employees from unfair deductions.

Why Should a Company Have a Disciplinary Policy?

A disciplinary policy explains what misconduct is and how the company will deal with it. It should cover warning, show cause notice, employee reply, inquiry, suspension where legally permissible, evidence review, final decision, and appeal or review mechanism where applicable. A disciplinary policy is important because punishment without process creates legal risk. If a company terminates an employee without notice, evidence, or inquiry in a serious misconduct case, the action may be challenged. A proper disciplinary policy helps the company act firmly but lawfully.

What Should Count as Misconduct in an HR Policy?

Misconduct should be defined clearly. It may include habitual absence, habitual late coming, negligence, disobedience of lawful instructions, harassment, violence, fraud, theft, breach of confidentiality, data misuse, misuse of company property, fake attendance, refusal to work, abusive behaviour, intoxication at workplace, or conduct harmful to the company. The policy should avoid vague language. “Bad attitude” is not enough. The misconduct should be connected with specific behaviour. A clear misconduct policy protects the employer from arbitrary decisions and protects the employee from surprise allegations.

Why Is a Show Cause Notice Procedure Important?

A show cause notice procedure is important because it gives the employee an opportunity to explain before punishment. It is also evidence that the employer followed due process. A proper HR policy should explain when a show cause notice will be issued, what it should contain, how much time will be given for reply, how evidence will be shared, and what happens if the employee does not respond. Why Is an Inquiry Procedure Necessary?

An inquiry procedure is necessary for serious or disputed allegations. It helps the employer examine facts before taking major action. A fair inquiry may include inquiry officer or committee, inquiry notice, evidence file, witness statements, employee defence, written proceedings, inquiry report, and final decision. Without inquiry, a termination based on misconduct may appear biased, rushed, or unsupported. A proper inquiry procedure strengthens the company’s legal position.

Why Does Every Company Need an Anti-Harassment Policy?

Every company should have an anti-harassment policy because workplace harassment is a serious legal and reputational issue. The policy should explain prohibited conduct, complaint procedure, inquiry committee, confidentiality, protection against retaliation, and consequences of harassment. A company that ignores harassment complaints may face legal action, employee turnover, reputational damage, and workplace instability. A clear anti-harassment policy protects employees and protects the company

Why Is a Code of Conduct Important?

A code of conduct defines professional behaviour. It should cover respect, communication, confidentiality, dress code where relevant, workplace dignity, non-discrimination, harassment prevention, conflict of interest, use of company property, social media conduct, and ethical duties. A code of conduct gives management a standard to enforce. It also gives employees a clear understanding of expected behaviour. A workplace without a code of conduct often becomes dependent on personal moods of managers.

Why Is a Company Property Policy Important?

Company property is a trust in the hands of employees. A company property policy should cover laptops, mobile phones, vehicles, keys, cards, cash, stock, documents, tools, uniforms, official SIMs, passwords, software access, and business records. It should explain issuance, permitted use, care obligations, damage reporting, loss reporting, return procedure, and consequences of misuse. This policy becomes very important when an employee resigns, is terminated, or refuses to return company assets.

Why Is a Data Protection and Confidentiality Policy Necessary?

Modern businesses depend on data. Customer lists, pricing, financial records, employee files, passwords, official emails, social media accounts, source code, website access, and client documents may be more valuable than physical assets. A proper HR policy should include confidentiality obligations, data access rules, password control, official email use, device security, cloud storage rules, social media access, and exit handover. Where office data is misused, the matter may involve employment misconduct, civil liability, cyber law, or criminal breach of trust depending on facts. Why Is a Mobile Phone and Internet Use Policy Important?

Many companies face productivity and discipline issues due to excessive mobile phone use, personal social media, private calls, entertainment browsing, and misuse of office internet. A mobile phone and internet policy should define what is allowed during duty hours, whether phones are restricted in sensitive areas, how official devices may be used, whether personal social media is allowed, and what conduct is prohibited. Without policy, termination for mobile phone misuse becomes difficult to defend.

Why Is a Workplace Complaint Policy Necessary?

Employees should have a safe and documented way to raise complaints. This may include complaints about harassment, abuse, salary deduction, unfair treatment, unsafe workplace, discrimination, forced resignation, or misconduct by another employee. A workplace complaint policy should explain who receives complaints, how complaints are recorded, how confidentiality is protected, how inquiry is conducted, and how retaliation is prevented. A good complaint policy reduces workplace conflict because employees know where to go before the matter becomes litigation.

Why Is a Final Settlement Policy Important?

Many disputes arise after resignation or termination. Employees claim unpaid salary, leave encashment, commission, overtime, gratuity, provident fund, notice pay, or withheld dues. Employers claim company property, notice period, loss, or incomplete handover. A final settlement policy should explain exit clearance, notice period, resignation acceptance, handover, asset return, final dues calculation, clearance certificate, confidentiality after exit, and full and final settlement. This helps close employment relationships professionally.

Why Should HR Policy Be Linked With Employment Contracts?

The HR policy and employment contract should support each other. The contract defines individual terms. The HR policy defines general workplace rules. If both documents conflict, disputes may arise. For example, if the contract allows one month notice but HR policy says immediate termination for any issue, the inconsistency may create legal risk. Businesses should have both documents reviewed together.

Can a Copied HR Policy Create Legal Risk?

Yes. A copied HR policy can create serious risk. Many companies copy policies from foreign websites, multinational templates, or unrelated industries. These policies may not match Pakistani law, local labour practices, company size, employee categories, or actual workplace needs. A policy that the company cannot implement is dangerous. It creates expectations but not compliance. A good HR policy should be legally aligned and practically enforceable.

How Often Should a Company Review Its HR Policy?

A company should review its HR policy regularly, especially when laws change, the business expands, new departments are created, remote work begins, digital systems are added, harassment complaints arise, or repeated disputes occur. A policy drafted once and forgotten for years becomes weak. Good companies treat HR policy as a living compliance document.

What Happens If a Company Has No HR Policy?

If a company has no HR policy, it may face disputes over hiring, salary, leave, attendance, discipline, harassment, termination, confidentiality, company property, and final settlement. The company may also struggle to prove that employees knew the rules. This can weaken disciplinary action and increase litigation risk. Lack of policy also creates inconsistent treatment. That may lead to claims of discrimination, victimization, bias, or unfair practice.

How Does HR Policy Improve Company Culture?

A proper HR policy improves culture because it creates predictability. Employees know the rules. Managers know limits. HR knows procedure. Directors know risk. Everyone understands what is acceptable and what is not. A fair policy reduces fear and confusion. It also prevents abuse of authority because management decisions must follow written standards. Clear rules create stronger workplaces.

How Does HR Policy Help During Litigation?

If an employment dispute reaches a labour forum, civil court, or other authority, documents become critical. A written HR policy helps show that the company had rules, communicated them, applied them, and acted according to process. It can support the employer’s position in disputes involving misconduct, salary deduction, termination, harassment, confidentiality breach, data misuse, or company property recovery. In litigation, memory is weak. Documents are strong.

What Should a Small Business Include in Its First HR Policy?

A small business should begin with practical essentials: Appointment process. Probation. Office timing. Attendance. Leave. Salary payment. Code of conduct. Mobile phone use. Confidentiality. Company property. Complaint procedure. Disciplinary process. Resignation. Termination. Final settlement. As the company grows, the policy can be expanded.

Final Legal Position

Every company needs a proper HR policy because employment disputes are not solved safely through verbal instructions. A written HR policy creates clarity, fairness, discipline, compliance, and legal protection. It protects employers by defining rules and procedures. It protects employees by defining rights and complaint mechanisms. It protects the business by reducing disputes, improving documentation, and strengthening workplace governance. The safest rule is simple: if the company expects employees to follow rules, those rules should be written, lawful, communicated, and consistently applied. A proper HR policy is not an expense. It is legal risk management.
